Scientific breakthroughs require research. Research at its core is sifting through information that already exists in order to make connections that haven't previously been made. And if you're lucky, you make enough of these connections to be able to perceive things differently in such a way so as to probe the unknown of reality with more effectiveness.

Learning how to research is essentially learning how to filter through information in a way to be able to see what you have not seen before, and ultimately, see what others have not seen before.

People generally never think about doing research in a way where they need to focus on improving themselves in order to facilitate their capacity for research.

You have to be able to sift through information in a way that'll allow you to see what hasn't been seen yet. How do you learn to get good at finding colors you've never seen before? Or have thought to even exist?
